Do independently trained models share causal variables?

A progress brief for #42 (causal convergence across seeds), 22 November 2026. Two findings: models trained apart, up to four 7–9B families, compute the same causal variable; and the cross-model transfer test that shows this passes just as well where a model does not use the variable. The full lab record is a separate page.

The short version

  • Pillar 1: models trained apart compute the same variable. The IOI variable ("which name was repeated") moves between independently trained models through a 16×16 rotation and one label-free scale, on every rung from Pythia-160M seeds to four 7–9B families (OLMo-3, Llama-3.1, Qwen2.5, Gemma-2): median transfer ratio T 0.94–1.02, and every rung's gap over random rotations has a 95% CI above 0 (Figure 1). At every scale it sits where the model itself reads it.
  • Same organ, different wiring. The heads and layers that carry the variable differ from seed to seed (top-10 head overlap 0.25 across seeds, 0.82 within one). What is shared is the causal state in the residual stream.
  • Pillar 2: passing transfer is not evidence of use. Transfer passes just as well where the model's own activation carries nothing: at 14 of 14 strictly off-route cells on 5 syntactic tasks at 160M and 3 of 3 at 1B (T 0.92–1.00). A cross-model transfer test shows shared writable geometry; whether the model reads it needs a separate check.
  • Not shown. Variables beyond IOI and short syntactic tasks; models beyond 9B; a second natural variable with more than two values.
  • Decision. Write #42 around these two pillars. Every run, figure and amendment behind this page is in the full lab record.

The question

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis says that separately trained models converge in representational geometry. #42 asks whether they also converge in the causal variables they compute with, and whether the test used to show that can be trusted. Inside each model, distributed alignment search finds a 16-dimensional subspace that carries a variable; between two models, a 16×16 rotation (times one scale) fitted without causal labels aligns the subspaces; the variable is read out of one model and written into the other. The design follows Model Alignment Search; every test below was preregistered.

1. Pillar 1: the variable transfers, from 160M seeds to 9B families

The main variable is IOI: in "When Mary and John went to the store, John gave a drink to …", which name was repeated. Each rung of the ladder changes one more thing between the two models: the random seed, then initialization versus data order, size, family and training corpus, scale (1B), and finally four 7–9B families with different tokenizers, widths and depths.

  • The learned map is the only thing that carries it. At the preregistered site, the learned rotation reaches 0.71 interchange accuracy against 0.39 for the 95th percentile of random rotations; identity, sign-flipped and mismatched-pairing maps stay low (Figure 1 of the lab record).
  • Nothing about training history matters. Sharing the initialization or the data order adds nothing once the destination model is fixed, and changing the corpus or the family leaves T at 0.9–1.0 (step 1, rung 3).
  • The sites are natural. At the selected site, transplanting the model's own activation carries the variable: natural lift 0.73–1.00 from 160M to 9B (lift ≥ 0.30 counts as natural).

Same organ, different wiring

An unsupervised check agrees. A crosscoder (one shared sparse dictionary over seven seeds) recovers 95% of the next-token loss of two seeds it never saw, against 97% for their own SAEs, and the same latent has the closest-matching causal effect in another seed 24% of the time against 1.7% chance (details).

Geometry can't see it

2. Pillar 2: passing transfer is not evidence of use

A transfer test writes a variable into the destination model. It cannot tell whether the destination model itself reads the variable there. Two checks separate the cases: the natural-site check (does transplanting the model's own activation carry the variable?) and Tanyel and Ayvaz's Algorithm 1 (is the cell's own effect G* above 0 in any context?).

  • Syntactic variables (CausalGym). Every task that localizes transfers between seeds: 27 of 27, or 24 of 24 after removing three tasks the models do at chance. But at 16 of 18 end-only sites the model's own activation moves the answer only to about chance while DAS's patch installs the source value: these sites are excess-dominated. They transfer anyway, also from Pythia to OLMo (median T 0.96) and from 160M to 410M (details, across families).

What pillar 2 says. Passing a cross-model subspace transfer test shows that the models share a geometry DAS can write into, not that the destination model computes with that variable at that site. Several 2026 papers use transfer across models as evidence of a shared variable; the check they need is the natural-site or G* test. A second caveat for the method itself: the usual localization gate (within IIA minus ablation IIA) favours cells the model does not read (Diagram D), so it should not be used as evidence that a subspace matters.

3. Method in one paragraph

DAS fits a rank-16 subspace per model at one site (a layer and token). The revised estimator (v2), fixed before it was used, trains on 792 prompts with validation early stopping and maps between models with a rotation times one scale c = rmsdestination / rmssource, computed from unlabeled activations; the random-rotation null gets the same c. The scale is what makes transfer across sizes and families symmetric (sizes and families differ in activation scale by up to two orders of magnitude). It is not what makes off-route cells transfer: at the 14 off-route cells, a rotation alone gives the same learned accuracy as rotation × scale (median 0.85 vs 0.85). The preregistered Pythia-160M numbers (rotation only, v1) remain the record (estimator details).

5. Limits

  • Task scope. IOI and short syntactic variables. Whether multi-step reasoning or long-context variables align this way is untested.
  • One natural variable with many values. After the natural-site check, IOI is the only natural non-binary variable; CausalGym's natural sites are all agreement, which is binary. Greater-than and name identity never localized.
  • Scale. Up to 9B, and independent seeds only up to 1B: no public suite has independently seeded pretraining above that.
  • Novelty. Tanyel and Ayvaz already show DAS's excess on CausalGym; cross-family transfer is known to work once the map is accurate. New here: transfer passes at excess-dominated and off-route sites, so transfer cannot certify use; and the seed-to-family ladder on real LMs.
