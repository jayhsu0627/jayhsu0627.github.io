The question

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis says that models trained separately converge in representational geometry. #42 asks whether they also converge in the causal variables they compute with. If they do, an interpretability finding in one model is a finding about all of them. If they don't, every circuit is a finding about one checkpoint.

The test, in four steps:

  1. Inside each seed, distributed alignment search finds a 16-dimensional subspace that carries the IOI variable (which name was repeated).
  2. Between two seeds, a 16×16 rotation aligns the two subspaces. It is fit without any causal labels.
  3. The variable is read out of seed A, rotated, and written into seed B.
  4. Interchange accuracy counts how often B's answer then flips the way the counterfactual says it should.

The design follows Model Alignment Search, run on the nine PolyPythias seeds. Seeds 1–2 set the thresholds; seeds 3–9 are confirmatory.

5. New: ladder step 1, initialization vs data order

A PolyPythias seed changes two things at once: the starting weights and the order the data arrives in. EleutherAI also released 160M runs that change only one of them, so this step asks which one the result depends on.

  • The step-0 weights confirm the split. The three data-seed models start from exactly the original model's weights.
  • The original weight-seed releases are broken; their model card says a training error continued them from the original weights. I used the two corrected ones that exist.No corrected weight-seed 3 is published, so the initialization-only group has 3 models and 6 directed pairs. The step-0 check also shows that PolyPythias seed1 has exactly the same initialization as weight-seed1-corrected.

At first glance, the initialization-only group transfers worse: its gap is -0.14 below data-only (95% CI -0.26 to -0.09). That difference belongs to the destination models, not to the sources. The two corrected weight seeds are the least accurate models in the set. Ablating the variable alone already flips many of their prompts, which raises both their baseline and their random null.

Comparing sources within the same destination removes that confound (Figure 5).

What it means. Initialization and data order are not where the shared variable comes from. Either difference alone is enough to scatter heads and layers: models with identical starting weights selected layers 4, 4, 5 and 5. Every model here read the same Pile, so what they share is the data distribution, the architecture and the objective. The next ladder steps are built to vary exactly those.

6. New: ladder step 2, a bigger model

The same test on nine Pythia-410M seeds (24 layers, 1,024 wide), plus transfer in both directions between 160M and 410M. The 16×16 rotation lives between the two 16-dimensional subspaces, so the different widths need no special handling. Everything below was preregistered before any 410M model was downloaded, except Figure 7.

  • Competence. Eight 410M seeds score 0.95–1.00 on IOI. Seed 4 scores 0.51, which is chance; it stays in, as preregistered.
  • Layers. The selected 410M layers run from 9 to 16 of 24, a wider relative spread than in 160M.
  • Reproduction. The fixed-layer 160M rows rerun inside this step match the frozen 160M result to four decimals (learned 0.7147).

The two directions look very different. 160M patches barely move 410M (T = 0.31), while 410M patches over-shoot 160M's own fit (T = 1.09). A rotation keeps length, and the DAS coordinates in 410M are larger: their spread is 6.0–6.8 in five 410M seeds against 2.6–4.7 in 160M, a median ratio of 1.48. Adding one scale factor, computed from unlabeled activations just like the rotation and applied to the random null as well, makes the two directions symmetric (Figure 7).

What it does not fix. Several 410M seeds that solve the task have a low own-model ceiling (seed 3: 0.17). Training the DAS map four times longer does not help: the ceiling falls by 0.11–0.23 in four 410M seeds while 160M barely moves, which looks like overfitting 72 training prompts in 1,024 dimensions. More training prompts, validation early stopping, rank, and a variable spread beyond S2 are untested. Section 7 tests the first two.

7. New: a revised estimator (v2)

Step 2 left two problems: a rotation cannot absorb the scale difference between 160M and 410M, and the own-model DAS fit overfits its 72 training prompts at 410M. Before running anything, I wrote down a revised estimator in the proposal:

  • DAS trains on 792 prompts instead of 72, and stops early on 792 validation prompts. Test names and the 1,092 test prompts do not change.
  • The map is the rotation times one scale factor, computed from unlabeled activations. The random rotations get the same factor.
  • v2 is the estimator for every later rung (DataDecide, OLMo), whatever these reruns showed. The v1 numbers above stay the record.

Every task that localizes also transfers, in all six families. Two facts change how to read this:

  • Most selected sites are lexical. At 182 of 203 model × task choices the site is the differing word. A follow-up the batch raised. The whole-space rotation behind H3 (scorecard) was fitted on about 800 task activations. That is too few to pin down a 768×768 rotation, so its failure might say nothing about the seeds. I refitted the rotation on up to 260k Pile tokens, with the reading written down before running.

Reading. T = 0.54 falls between the preregistered thresholds (0.50 and 0.80), so it supports no summary claim. By family: agreement 0.71, subordination 0.66, cleft 0.56, garden path 0.49, npi 0.46, filler–gap 0.43. Against simply ablating the variable, the global rotation adds -0.01 (median).

This looked like the surprise. Section 9 explains it. At these sites the seeds look nearly identical as wholes (debiased CKA 0.93–0.97). The variables transfer perfectly once matched locally, yet no global rotation carries them. Three more preregistered follow-ups tracked down why. For most of these tasks, the answer is a known weakness of the method, not a property of the seeds.

9. New: at most syntactic sites DAS overshoots the model, and the IOI subspace does travel

Follow-up 2: linear maps and in-distribution text. A general linear map (least squares) does no better than a rotation. The best global linear map gives T = 0.55 when fitted on Pile and 0.45 when fitted on the task sentences themselves, and no task reaches 0.80. By the preregistered reading, this is the candidate cell.

The geometry did not fit that story. The task-text map sends seed A's class-mean difference onto seed B's almost exactly: cosine 0.85 at end-only and 0.985 at the selected site, 1.00 on all four NPI tasks. Yet on those NPI tasks the transfer through it is near zero. The map gets what the variable looks like right, but not the subspace the interchange acts on.

Follow-up 3: move the value, not the subspace. Use the global map only to translate seed A's activation into seed B's coordinates, and write it into B's own DAS subspace. Now the variable transfers: T = 0.89 at end-only and 1.00 at the selected site. The value travels between seeds through one global map. What fails is carrying A's subspace across.

The natural-site check (first called an illusion check). A smoke test showed something odd. On NPI, transplanting the seed's own whole activation from the source sentence did nothing, while DAS's 16-dimensional patch of that same activation flipped almost every prompt. A patch that beats the whole activation it was cut from is the signature of the subspace-patching illusion: it pushes a direction that downstream layers read but that real activations never move. It is also the signature of DAS overshooting a route the model does use, which the correction below shows is what happens here. I preregistered a check before the full run. A site counts as natural if the real activation's lift over ablation is at least 0.30.

  • What it means for CausalGym. At these sites the model's own activation moves the answer, but only to about chance; DAS's 16-dimensional patch installs the source value. "27 of 27 transfer" stands as measured, but on 23 tasks the transfer goes into a subspace whose effect is mostly excess over what the destination's real activations do. So cross-seed transfer alone does not certify a variable the model uses, which applies to any transfer test of this kind. Restricted to natural sites, CausalGym adds the agreement family (T = 0.99), not 27 variables.
  • What it means for the local-vs-global gap. At excess-dominated sites the gap has a mundane explanation. A DAS subspace includes a part that real activations never exercise, so no map fitted on activations can place it, while B's own subspace (the hybrid) works. Exploratory: across tasks, the more natural the site, the better the global map transfers (Spearman +0.61).
  • What it means for IOI. The IOI sites are natural: the real S2 activation's lift is +0.73 at the frozen 160M site and +0.92 at 410M, above the DAS patch's. The headline result passes this check.

Correction, 19 Nov. The first version of this section called the non-natural sites "illusions" and said the model "doesn't use" the variable there. A reconciliation with Tanyel and Ayvaz shows that overstates it. Our natural lift is measured against ablating the DAS subspace, which sits near chance (median 0.48). Measured against the unpatched run, as they do, the real activation's effect is clearly positive at most of these sites (median G +0.26). In their terms, 14 of 16 end-only tasks are excess on the route, and only 2 have G ≈ 0. Also, three CausalGym tasks are at chance behaviorally (cleft_mod, filler_gap_embed_3, filler_gap_embed_4; clean accuracy 0.50–0.52), yet they pass the gate and count in "27 of 27". Follow-up 5: is the carried subspace causal in B at all? Write B's own source value into A's subspace as the global map places it in B. That gives T = 0.29 at best, against 0.09 for a random 16-dimensional subspace. The carried subspace shares about a tenth of B's own (overlap 0.16, against 0.02 at random). B's subspace is not a dormant direction: it holds 18× the variance of an average subspace.

That looked like a finding: one global map carries what the IOI variable's value is, but not where each seed's computation reads it. It survived the controls for rotation vs linear map, fitting text, and the illusion. One alternative remained: the map is clearly imprecise at S2.

The candidate is withdrawn. By the preregistered rule, one held-out map carries A's subspace well enough: T = 0.85, from the crosscoder's linear approximation. That condition is odd on its own, because B's own value on the same subspace reaches only 0.44. The oracle settles it. A linear map that predicts the S2 activations accurately carries the causal subspace: T = 0.96, and B's own value on it 0.95 (at least 0.80 in 39 of 42 pairs). Across maps, the more accurate the map, the more causal the subspace it carries (Spearman 0.75). So the seeds do share the causal subspace, up to a linear map. What follow-ups 4 and 5 measured is an accuracy requirement: carrying the value tolerates a poor map, carrying the subspace needs a nearly exact one.

  • Overlap doesn't measure "the same subspace". The oracle's carried subspace shares only 0.09 of B's DAS subspace, yet it is fully causal in B. Many 16-dimensional subspaces of B work, so DAS subspaces are not identifiable and follow-up 5's overlap reading is superseded.
  • Why held-out maps fall short. S2 is a name token. A map fitted on other names explains only 36% of the variance at the test names, and no held-out map reached the oracle's accuracy.
  • The crosscoder can't carry an edit. Even a round trip into the same seed keeps only 0.32 of the variable, so its exact read/write was uninformative, as fixed in advance.

10. New: the crosscoder arm

DAS tests variables we choose. The crosscoder asks the reverse question across thousands of unlabeled features: one shared sparse dictionary over layer 5 of seeds 1–7, with seeds 8 and 9 held out. Every causal test encodes each seed from its own activations only.

Option P: why ablation flips most answers at the last token (exploratory). Each entry is IIA, with the median logit difference z (source label minus base label) in parentheses. At the last token, the answer is linear in the subspace coordinates: z after mean ablation equals the midpoint of z at the base's own value-class mean and at the other class's mean, to within 0.1 logit. The subspace moves z by ±2.9 to ±5.4 logits, while the rest of the residual adds a constant 1.6–3.4 logits toward whichever value the base does not have. Normally the subspace wins; ablating or randomizing it leaves the rest's push, so the answer flips. This is DAS excess seen from the ablation side: the subspace's swing is about twice the model's natural effect, and the model's own state cancels part of it elsewhere.   • Seed 9. It does the task but spreads the variable across layers. A fixed-layer analysis understates conservation there.
  • Novelty (literature check, 18 Nov; doc/related_work.md). Model Alignment Search transfers causal variables across seeds, but only in toy networks. McCann carries SAEs across Pythia-70M seeds with one global rotation, which conflicts with our IOI result. Three papers from the last month narrow what is new:
    • Tanyel and Ayvaz (3 Nov) already compare DAS with whole-state patching on CausalGym and find DAS's excess at most live cells. Our natural-site check is partly known, and run A agrees with them: our sites are excess on the route. Our gate, rank 16, nine seeds, the IOI contrast and the transfer test are not.
    • Generic-text maps carry a noun-number direction across 20 models from five families.
    • Transfer across model families works once the map covers the tool's region, which is follow-up 6's lesson.
    Still new: transfer between seeds, families and sizes passes at excess-dominated sites, and between seeds at off-route cells (option L), so transfer does not certify a variable the model uses, which is the evidence these 2026 papers rely on. Also new: the seed ladder on real LMs. The closest ICLR 2027 submission, "Cross-Model Stitching Transfers Answers, Not Computation" (98Rbm5C5Bl), makes a related point with stitching.
